# 6. Royalties and Paperwork

The royalties system is the part of KDP beginners fear most, because it involves banks, tax forms, and a two-month delay that feels wrong until you understand it. This chapter walks through the money side in plain English: how royalties flow, what forms you'll fill out, what records to keep, and what to hand to a professional.

> **Education, not legal, tax, or accounting advice.** This chapter explains the system and your *record-keeping obligations to yourself* — not your specific tax situation. For anything with legal or money consequences — entity choice, tax filing, deductions — talk to a qualified professional. We are a *publishing guide*, and this chapter stops exactly where your accountant's job begins.

## How royalties actually flow (and the two-month delay)

KDP pays royalties **about 60 days after the end of the month in which the sale happened**. A sale on March 2 earns royalties paid in a payment in early June. Here's the timeline:

1. **A sale happens** in month M. The dashboard tracks the units and estimated royalties in near real time.
2. **The month closes.** Around the middle of month M+1, the prior month's royalty report is finalized in KDP's reports.
3. **KDP issues payment around 60 days after** the sale month, to the bank account and payee you set up. **The 60-day window admits returns and adjustments to process** — which is exactly why the number in your dashboard and the number in your bank rarely match on any given day.

Two numbers, always distinct, and always confusing to beginners:

- **Units (sales) count** — how many copies sold in the period. Note: a *sale* in the dashboard is not *money in the bank*. Not yet, and not the full price either.
- **Royalty earned** — your per-unit royalty from Chapter 3's math, summed. That's the number that eventually arrives. (And the *money* that arrives is what's yours. The list price was never yours.)

Returns of paperbacks and ebooks subtract from royalty, too — a returned ebook doesn't earn you a royalty, and a returned paperback goes back into sellable stock without a print-cost refund. It's normal; build it into your mental model and move on.

## Getting set up to be paid: the forms

KDP requires **tax identity information** before it pays anyone. (Also before some features unlock.) What you fill out depends on where you and your business are located.

### If you're publishing as a US person or entity

- **Form W-9** — the standard request for taxpayer identification. You (or your LLC) supply name, address, taxpayer ID (for an LLC, typically the EIN).
- Name and taxpayer ID must match **exactly** how the IRS knows you/your entity. A single mismatch can hold up payments.
- Publishing as your business (LLC or sole prop)? **Then the tax info you enter should be the business's info**, and the bank account should be the business's account. This keeps your books clean and matches how the LLC's income is reported.

### If you're publishing from outside the US

- **Form W-8BEN (individual)** or **W-8BEN-E (entity)** establishes non-US status and treaty benefits. KDP has an **interview-style flow** in the dashboard that generates the right form for your situation; you largely answer questions.
- Royalties may be subject to **US withholding tax** unless a treaty reduces it. This is treaty-dependent and personal — read your country's treaty summary page on the IRS site, and confirm with a professional. The point for you: non-US authors *do* get paid on KDP routinely; the forms exist for exactly this reason.

### Payment details

- You pick a bank account for deposits. US authors typically get US bank transfers (with optional wire for foreign banks); foreign authors reach KDP's supported-market list. Verify your bank's supported conditions early, and set this **before launch** so the first royalty cycle isn't stuck waiting on an incomplete setup.

## The LLC corner: doing it right the first time

Publishing "as yourself" vs. "as your LLC" determines whose name appears as publisher and whose tax identity receives the money. This matters if, like ours, your plan includes multiple books and tools under a business name:

- **Publisher name on the copyright page.** "© 2026 Asdale Dynamics, LLC. All rights reserved." — that's the entity, clearly identified.
- **The KDP account owner.** You can publish from a personal account or re-register as a business. Keep the account/payee/tax identity **consistent with your copyright page**, so that your paper trail — book copyright, KDP account, payment, taxes — all point at the same thing.
- **Bank account.** An LLC's royalty income flowing into a personal account is a bookkeeping and liability-separation habit worth fixing *before* the first payment lands, not after.

LLC vs. sole proprietorship vs. doing it personally is a **professional's question**, not a guide's. Once it's decided, the record-keeping below applies in either case.

## Record-keeping that you'll thank yourself for

For each sale period, keep:

1. **KDP's monthly royalty statements** (downloadable from the dashboard, PDF or TSV). These are the ledger of record. Save them to a `royalties/` folder, by month.
2. **A simple running log**: month, units sold, royalty earned, amount received to bank. Columns take ten minutes a month and turn "did I actually get paid for March?" from a mystery into a lookup.
3. **Expense receipts**: proof copy order, ISBN purchase (if you bought one), editing, cover art, ads. Business expenses on your *own* records at the time of purchase — reconstructing them at tax time is the classic mistake.
4. **Bank statement match**: when the payment lands, mark the row in your log as *received* and note the date. If it's late by a week or two, that's normal timing; not a problem to chase yet.

This is genuinely all the "accounting system" a first KDP author needs: a folder of statements, one spreadsheet, and a receipts folder. It scales fine through your third or fourth book.

## A note on ISBNs

For paperbacks, KDP offers a **free KDP-assigned ISBN** or lets you supply your own purchased ISBN. For most first books, the **free KDP ISBN** is the sensible default: it works, it's free, and buying an ISBN (or block) matters most if you have plans to publish the same print edition through other channels/retailers later under *your* imprint. Don't buy ISBNs you don't have a concrete plan for. The ebook doesn't use an ISBN on KDP — it gets an **ASIN** (Amazon's internal product identifier).

## What stays and what goes: a short keep-forever list

- [ ] Copyright page text (and the year-of-first-publication date, in case of later editions).
- [ ] All monthly royalty statements, forever.
- [ ] The payment log spreadsheet, forever.
- [ ] Receipts for every business expense, for the retention period your tax professional recommends (often years — ask them).
- [ ] The final manuscript files and the final interior/cover uploads, in a labeled folder. Your *next* edition's starting point is this archive; losing it means re-typesetting from a stale draft.

## Where the professional's job starts

- Choosing LLC vs. sole prop, and *when* to form one.
- Whether and how royalties are deductible business expenses, VAT/sales taxes on a book in your state/country.
- Annual filings for your entity.
- Any cross-border/treaty question more complex than the standard W-8 form interview.

KDP has help pages titled "Tax Information" and "Getting Paid" that restate the mechanics officially — read them once through before your first payment cycle so nothing about the timeline surprises you.
