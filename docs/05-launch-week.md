# 5. Launch Week

A first-book launch does not need a countdown, a launch team, a free-run to #1, or a week of your life in panic mode. Those rituals exist because they photograph well on social media and because the folklore that surrounds KDP promotes them.

This chapter describes the low-stress launch that protects your reputation and your energy, and the five things actually worth watching in the first seven days.

## The goal of launch week

Not "hit number one." The real goals of a first-book launch week:

1. **Confirm quality.** Find any formatting or content error a real reader encounters, and have a plan to fix it fast, with pride-free humility nobody will fault you for.
2. **Create a truthful, public record.** The book *exists*, correctly described, at its real price, from day one. That record ages well.
3. **Stay under your own energy budget.** Launch weeks that require 14-hour days are exactly the reason "low-stress" belongs in this guide's title.

That's it. A calm, quietly competent launch week is worth more than a frenzied one that ends with regret.

## The week *before* launch (prep, not panic)

- **Order the paperback proof** and read every page in print, out of order. On-screen preview misses things a physical copy never does.
- **Run two full read-throughs of the Kindle preview** — one on the desktop previewer, one on your phone's Kindle app. Note every formatting grievance, however small, in a list.
- **Fix your errata list** before publishing; you'll find it's short. Publishing with known errors you plan to "fix later" is the same as shipping broken on purpose.
- **Have your description, keywords, categories, and price settled** (Chapters 3 and 4). Don't touch a single one after clicking publish except to fix a genuine error.
- **Pre-write one social post** announcing the book — warm, plain, one screen — and one email if you have a list, mentioning the book and its utility, honestly. Anything beyond that in launch week is optional.

For *The Massive AI Manual*: I published without any launch ritual at all. No launch team, no banner ads, no free-run. The steady post-launch warm simmer of people finding a good book, and finding the description, exactly as promised, is what genuinely moves the numbers over the long tail.

## The seven days, day by day (roughly)

- **Day 0 (publish):** Click publish, then do *nothing else* related to marketing. Verify the live listing: title, author name, price, categories, description, and especially the **Look Inside** — sample pages render correctly, TOC shows up, no stray garbage. If anything's broken, fix it today in the dashboard or by re-uploading the corrected file.
- **Days 1–2:** Read the sales dashboard once. Note anything odd. **Do not refresh it hourly.** Answer any early reader message or question you get, honestly and warmly — but don't hunt reviews; real ones take their time.
- **Days 3–5:** Continue in quiet mode. If a real reader reaches a problem or typo in your pages: **add it to an errata log** immediately. If it's a formatting issue affecting the reading experience — a paragraph break, an image, a broken TOC — fix and re-upload. Faults no one would care about go into the log too, but put them in the *next* revision batch, not a same-day emergency upload.
- **Day 6–7:** Ask first readers, privately and personally, to leave an *honest* review when ready — including positive and negative. Not now, not in bulk — later is fine and better.

## What NOT to do in launch week

- **No purchased services** promising reviews, rankings, "agent air traffic" — all of that is against KDP's terms and it *will* eventually come for the account, or worse, the account's owner.
- **No declaring sales on social media every day.** No #18 in an obscure category badge worth little.
- **No price games.** No flash discount and re-listings. Set the right price (Chapter 3) and leave it.
- **No last-minute content changes.** Post-launch edits should be for discovered defects, not for "I felt it needed another paragraph." Every re-upload resets some review-dependent parts of the funnel and risks surprising your reviewers' Look Inside with different text.
- **No launching books 2, 3, and 4 in the same week.** You'll be tempted when the dopamine hits. It'll still be a good idea next month.

## Realistic expectations, in one table

| What a calm launch realistically looks like | What it does *not* look like |
| --- | --- |
| First sales trickle in from the audience you already have, word of mouth, and search | A chart that spikes to bestseller badge within 24 hours |
| Errata arrives gradually; you log it, fix the important parts | A perfect, perfect book you never touch again |
| You worked a normal week, slept, maybe read a real book | You monitored the dashboard every 30 minutes for seven days |
| A couple of early honest reviews appear over the weeks that follow | Fifty five-star reviews appear in the first 48 hours (the KDP system flags this pattern) |

The quiet, boring launch is the one that's actually survivable, and *survivable* is the property you want in every piece of your first publishing experience. Serial publishing requires you to still enjoy this on book five.
